Our story

The poor are not
a market.

They are neighbours.

{% include "illustrations/_well.html" with class="w-full h-auto" %}

Everyone is searching for the same thing — something that doesn't move. Christian or atheist, president or peasant: all of us are looking for somewhere secure to stand.

So you build something a person can turn to the moment they feel lost, and you fill it with the one thing that doesn't run out: each other.

{# The two commandments, as a visual beat #}
One act, not two

Love God with everything you are.

All your time, your thoughts, your hope — and your actions.

 

Love your neighbour as yourself.

When you give to the poor, you give to God. Charity is love with its sleeves rolled up.

And when the person you helped wants to give something back, you let them. Iron sharpens iron. Nobody here is only a giver, and nobody is only a case. It's Acts, chapter four: a community that held everything in common, so that no one among them was in need. Not a program run for them — a thing they run for each other.

{# Pull-quote — the line that carries the mission #} {# Why it got built — beside the exchange scene #}
{% include "illustrations/_exchange.html" with class="w-full h-auto" %}

I built the first version of this because I was tired of watching love get turned into logistics — good people ready to help, and no dignified way for the help to find the need.

So the rule underneath everything is consent, not surveillance. Your community's information stays in your community's hands. The software is built so that, when someone asks to be forgotten, it can actually forget them. That is the reason the thing exists.

We started with a parish notice-board because you have to start with something real — something you can point at and say, here, look, it works.
